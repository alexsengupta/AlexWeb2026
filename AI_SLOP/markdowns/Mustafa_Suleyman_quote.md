# Twelve Months to Obsolescence? 

This  one isn’t an article its a quote from a recent Financial Times interview by Microsoft’s AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman, plus a few of my own thoughts

“Human level performance on most if not all professional tasks - white collar work where you're sitting down at a computer either being a lawyer or an accountant or a project manager or marketing person. Most of those tasks will be fully automated by an AI within the next 12 to 18 months and we can see this in software engineering. Many software engineers report that they are now using AI assisted coding for the vast majority of their code. Reduction, which means that their roles shifted now to this meta function of debugging, scrutinizing, of doing the strategic stuff like architecting of, etc, putting things into production. So it's a quite different relationship to the technology and that's happened in the last six months”.
(Microsoft’s AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman)

Of course this could be just hype - many people certainly think this. And Suleyman isn’t a disinterested commentator: his company needs to attract customers and pay for the huge costs of AI. Whats more theres a big difference between software engineering and many other white collar jobs, that may be trickier to replace. But many other leaders in AI including the heads of Anthropic and DeepMind (the two I would probably have most trust in) are saying similar things (although the time line in the case of Demis Hassabis is a few years longer)

And these are the people that see what the cutting edge looks like and whats likely to be next

What would this mean if this were an accurate prediction? Would this portend the utopia that the AI cheer leaders talk about. As a glass half empty person I think not. Leaders of industry with the option to massively reduce their labour costs, will do just that. Of course this wont happen overnight, there is massive inertia on our socioeconomic system - but this seems like the most likely trajectory given the incentives of companies to make profit.
 The winners will be those leaders that can access and afford the greatest amount of AI time, plus some at the top of the game who who can fill the gaps that AI cannot (yet): setting high level goals, provide contextual judgement, and acting as a head to roll if something goes wrong. The liberating technology that was to bring knowledge and capability to all, will allow those with mass access to supercharge their wealth creation. The rest get UBI or worse. We dont have mechanisms to deal with this kind of disruption, occurring on such short timescales. What social unrest do we get when we have anccelerating unemployment of white collar workers. (And those that think their jobs are safe because they do manual work, take a look at Helix02 - the humanoid robots are not far behind).

I see the incredible advances that are being made with every release of the leading models. From a disquieting toy 3 years ago,  I now discuss my research ideas with AI systems , use it for all my coding, talk about ethical dilemmas. AI currently augments my productivity - which is exciting. But it’s increasingly capable at doing things independently when given a brief overarching directive from me  - things that give me meaning and that I dont want to hand over to a machine, like teaching or problem solving. Perhaps what scares me most is this loss of meaning, The claims of the tech leaders seem credible to me. While the arguments I hear from the AI sceptics, that AI wont be able to take over most cognative tasks, have been unconvincing to me. And I would really love to hear an argument that convinces me that this accelerating snowball can be controlled and wont crush our economy, social structures and meaning
