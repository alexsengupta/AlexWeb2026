Simulation Guide

Winds blowing over an ocean basin are the primary cause of ocean gyres and western boundary currents.

Surface winds drive Ekman transport that converge water in the centre of basins, causing sea surface gradients that drive the gyres. Changes in Coriolis with latitude and frictional effects mean that these gyres are asymmetric and form western boundary currents.

Welcome to the Stommel Basin Simulator. This interactive tool models ocean circulation in a simplified rectangular basin driven by wind stress.

How to Use

  • Visualize: Watch Sea Surface Height evolve. High pressure (red) generally corresponds to clockwise flow in the Northern Hemisphere.
  • Particles: Click anywhere on the map to release green drift particles to trace currents.
  • Controls: Use the panel on the right to adjust physical parameters in real-time.
  • Instability: If the simulation turns red and explodes, the numerical solver has become unstable. Increase viscosity or friction, then reset.

Things to Try

  • Western intensification: Set beta positive and note boundary current on the western edge.
  • f-plane: Set beta to 0 and observe symmetric gyres.
  • Southern Hemisphere: Set f0 negative and note the flow reversal.
  • Reverse beta: Set beta negative and watch intensification move east.

Model Physics

The model solves depth-averaged shallow water equations on a C-grid using a simplified finite-difference scheme.

// Momentum equations (linear)
du/dt - f v = -g d(eta)/dx + tau_x/(rho H) - r u + Ah laplacian(u)
dv/dt + f u = -g d(eta)/dy + tau_y/(rho H) - r v + Ah laplacian(v)

// Continuity equation
d(eta)/dt + d(Hu)/dx + d(Hv)/dy = 0

Key Terms

  • Coriolis (f): Deflects moving fluid. f = f0 + beta y.
  • Beta (beta): Variation of Coriolis with latitude, crucial for Sverdrup balance and western boundary currents.
  • Wind stress (tau): The engine of the circulation. Wind curl drives gyres.
  • Friction (r) and viscosity (Ah): Dissipate energy and allow a steady state to form.

The first two equations are an expression of Newton's law F = ma. They say that the acceleration of water relates to pressure forces (related to sea surface height gradients), wind forces, and frictional forces (at boundaries and internally).

The third equation tells us that volume is conserved: water entering a region must be balanced by water exiting some other region.

The equations represent the combined effects of Ekman and geostrophic currents.

Critically, this app shows us that:

  1. The interaction of wind forces and the Coriolis force can generate ocean gyres.
  2. We need to include the fact that the Coriolis force changes with latitude, together with frictional effects, to be able to understand western boundary currents.
